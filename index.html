MMIP Fundamentals: Noisy Speech Processing Pipeline

1. Speech & Noise Spectral Characteristics

Human speech energy is primarily concentrated in the formant range (300 Hz to 3400 Hz), with vowels carrying low-frequency harmonic fundamentals (F₀ ≈ 85–255 Hz) and unvoiced consonants (s, f, sh) extending up to 8 kHz.

  • Low-Pass Filter (LPF): Cuts high-frequency channel hiss (f > Fc), preserving fundamental speech formants.
  • High-Pass Filter (HPF): Cuts low-frequency AC mains hum (50/60 Hz) and DC wind rumble (f < Fc).

2. Butterworth IIR Filter Transfer Function

The Butterworth filter provides a maximally flat magnitude response in the passband without ripple:

|H(f)|² = 1 / [1 + (f / Fc)²ᴺ]

Implemented in real-time via second-order Direct Form II Transposed Biquad IIR Digital Filters:

y[n] = b₀·x[n] + b₁·x[n-1] + b₂·x[n-2] - a₁·y[n-1] - a₂·y[n-2]

3. Fourier Analysis & Spectrogram (STFT)

The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) decomposes non-stationary speech signals into localized time-frequency representations:

X(m, ω) = ∑ x[n] · w[n - m] · e⁻ʲʷⁿ

This allows visual verification of noise suppression across time frames in the spectrogram heatmap.
